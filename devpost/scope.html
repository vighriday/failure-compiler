Failure Compiler

A crash-test tool for plans: it tests every single failure and every pair from a fixed list, and shows you which ones break it before Saturday does.
Scope · Approved

Inspiration and Identity

Approved Landing v2: Overview, Plan, Read, Crash test, Trust and Start share the paper interface. The Start tab contains the editor. Source highlights follow the plan story on scroll; the pipeline can be replayed. The page-break effect restores every fragment and control, including when navigating away. All motion respects reduced motion. Illustrative previews are labelled; the confirmed-map flow runs the live checker.

The Unique Kernel

Most risk tools give you a list of general warnings. Failure Compiler finds the specific combination of two ordinary problems that breaks your specific plan, where each one alone would have been fine, and then tests whether a proposed fix actually works against every scenario that was tested, not just the one that broke. Problems that are invisible alone. Fixes that get tested before you trust them.
Why this matters

Plans look finished because every box is filled. Every task has a name. Every important job has a backup. So everyone relaxes.

But a backup only helps if it does not need the same thing as the person it is backing up. If your backup driver needs the same van, you do not really have a backup. And the worst problems usually are not the single obvious ones: they are two small things going wrong at once, where each alone would have been fine. Nobody checks those combinations, because there are too many to think through. That is exactly the kind of huge, boring job a computer is good at.

Who It's For

A volunteer coordinator running a Saturday morning food distribution for a community kitchen or food bank. They plan in a group chat and a spreadsheet. A handful of volunteers, one van, one car, one loading area, a hard finish time, because families are waiting.

Later: event organisers, small delivery teams, anyone whose plan depends on people, vehicles, places, and deadlines.

Five Screens

1
Paste Plan
A text area. No checklist yet: the "looks complete" view is on the plan map screen, where the AI reading is available.
2
Plan Map
At the top: the checklist showing what the plan looks like when every job has a person and a backup, all ticked. It looks done. Below: the AI's structured reading as short plain statements. Source tags distinguish the AI's citation for a particular field from an entity mention; a mention is not proof of that field's value. Missing field citations stay missing. Hover or focus a tag to highlight its own line. Guesses are marked and asked as questions. Constrained correction, rule validation, confirmation gate. AI does not re-read after a correction.

4
Broken Case Detail + Fixes
For a hidden pair: shows the two failures and that each alone was fine. The three example pairs are Supply truck late + Ravi absent; Van late + Anita leaves early; Van late + Anita absent. For a single weak spot: shows the one failure. Either way: a timeline of the morning. A not-delivered route is drawn as never leaving, with its reason, e.g. “Van back 10:45. Loading team gone.” Human cost: “Route A: 10 households not delivered,” “Route C: 8 households not delivered,” or “Routes A, B, C: 30 households not delivered. Nobody can open the loading area”. Plain one-sentence explanation.
Fix suggestions (up to 3): each shows fixes-N / breaks-N / new-crosses before acceptance. Selecting a fix previews its grid with changed cells outlined. New crosses are marked distinctly so they cannot hide. Accepting a fix updates the main plan and re-tests everything. Fixes stack: accept one, then another; each becomes part of the plan. One fix can contain more than one change. Before/After toggle compares original plan to current fixed version.
5
Report
Available after the first completed run, even with crosses left. Actual before/after counts, accepted AI/yours totals, suggestion verdict totals and main human cost open the report. Confirmed facts and source tags, exact tests and sizes, original breaks and dimmed groups, changes and scores, final results and explicit limits follow. The full example prints to five A4 or Letter pages with intact cards and a fingerprint footer.

The broken-case panel also offers "Test your own change": up to three fixed-choice operations through the same gate and all-scenario scorer. The actual report records three accepted fixes (one AI, two yours) and 12 AI suggestions (ten turned down, one not applicable, one accepted). Current visual design is final; keyboard, visible focus, reduced motion, text at least 12px and contrast at least 4.5:1 remain required.

Example Mode (No Key Required)

Example mode: using a saved AI reading

The example ships with one unedited Gemini read-v4 reading and four unedited Groq fallback suggestion responses. The reading has 39 reviewed field citations and 10 null citations; missing evidence stays a labelled mention. The full flow still runs: plan map, rules, questions and confirmation gate. The checker scores the confirmed plan live; previews and Before/After use stored results rather than re-solving. Saved mode makes no provider calls. Pasting your own plan requires a key.

The example plan has Route A with 10 households, Route B with 12, and Route C with 8. The van returns from an early pickup at 9:30; Joel has the only loading-area key and leaves at 10:30; Maya leaves at 10:45; Sam is not on the loading team. All three van + Anita leaves early AI cards fail; another driver cannot fix loading. The accepted Sam loader card fixes both van pairs. The AI's spare key for Ravi reveals Joel + Ravi absent and is turned down. Coordinator-built spare keys for Maya and Sam fix Joel; Sam as Route A backup fixes the truck pair. All fixes together give 100 survived. Five fixed problem types instantiate 14 example problems and 100 scenarios, with a full checker-run target below three seconds.

The 2:30 path in demo-runbook.md confirms the Hall identity question, guessed rows and four arrival questions, opens the main van cross, accepts Sam's loader card, then the two coordinator changes, and compares Before/After before the report. /demo resets only that browser before each take. The same confirmed map always gets the same verdicts.

The Validation Pipeline (The Learning Goal)

AI reads, rules check, human confirms, strict system acts. A repeatable pattern applicable to any "AI turns messy input into something a strict system acts on" pipeline.

AI
Parse messy plan into fixed-schema structured map; surface ambiguities as questions
Rules
Validate the map: every name exists, every route has a vehicle and driver, every time is coherent, nothing the checker needs is missing. Rule failures become questions, not silent errors.
Human
Confirm or correct using constrained choices only. Corrections go straight into the map; AI does not re-read.
Checker
Runs only once every item is confirmed and every rule passes. Deterministic. AI never has the final word.

The Checker's Three Honest Answers

✕
This breaks your plan. Here is exactly how.
✓
Your plan survived everything we tested. That is not the same as "your plan is safe." It only covers what we tested.
?
I could not tell. Something is missing or unclear. Never treated as a pass.
